Overview

The brief, and what I decided to do with it

The brand

Slow North is a small, woman-owned business in Austin, Texas making sustainable, non-toxic wellness products. Its core offer is a "Clean Burn" experience — natural fragrance blends built from soy wax and pure essential oils, plus hands-on candle-making workshops.

Home & living goods US market Sustainability DTC + workshops

The objective I set

  • Introduce the brand and its natural-ingredient story to a Vietnamese audience who had not encountered it.
  • Present the seasonal and festive candle line-up alongside the offer stack printed on the brochure — 50% off for webinar attendees, a 15% sign-up saving and 20% off in-store purchases.
  • Drive registrations through a single organic Facebook promotion, then measure whether the audience actually showed up and whether they were satisfied.
Process

Before, during, after — the agenda I ran to

Before the event

Planning · promotion · technical setup
  • Confirmed the topic, built the registration form, designed the brochure, and set the session timing.
  • Promoted the webinar on Facebook using the brochure creative — a three-panel design carrying the product story, the workshop tiers ($60–$100) and a QR code straight into the registration form — then tracked sign-ups against the 25 August deadline.
  • The post drew 28 reactions and 21 comments, mostly from a young audience, and held steady interaction across the registration window.
  • Created the event on Microsoft Teams Webinar and checked audio, video and notification settings before going live.
  • Set an automatic confirmation email to fire on every registration.
Brochure design Facebook promo MS Teams setup Email automation

During the event

Delivered live, in English
  • Ran a tight 30-minute session (22:30–23:00 GMT+7) — opening purpose, then the brand overview and mission.
  • Covered the ingredient story — soy wax and essential oils including lavender, lemon, jasmine, rosemary, cinnamon, sandalwood and cedar.
  • Presented seven signature products — Lavender Lemonade, Tuscan Sun, Bouquet, Hello Sunshine, Merry + Bright, Autumn Woods and Cabin Retreat — and the three workshop formats.
  • Shared contact routes (website, Instagram, address, hotline) and facilitated a live Q&A.
English presenting Product storytelling Q&A facilitation

After the event

Measurement · documentation
  • Sent a feedback survey the next morning; 13 of the 15 attendees responded — an 87% response rate.
  • Read the results on four axes: satisfaction, which workshop format they wanted, which scent they would buy first, and whether they would come back.
  • Evaluated whether the session met its objective and recorded what to change for a future run.
Survey design Satisfaction analysis Post-event review

What I would do differently

Honest review
  • One organic Facebook post carried the whole funnel — a small paid boost would have widened the top of it considerably.
  • I measured satisfaction but not workshop redemption; tracking how many attendees used the 50% offer would have closed the loop to revenue.
  • A reminder email the day before would likely have lifted the show-up rate above 88%.
Funnel thinking Conversion tracking
Results

What the campaign produced

The funnel

  • One organic Facebook post carried the whole top of funnel: 28 reactions, 21 comments, no paid spend behind it.
  • 17 people registered through the Teams form, each receiving an automated confirmation email with a join link and a cancel option.
  • 15 attended live on 27 August 2024 — an 88% show-up rate, high for a free online session scheduled late in the evening.
  • 13 attendees (87%) completed the post-event survey, which is what made the result measurable rather than anecdotal.

What the audience said

Survey question Result
How satisfied were you with the event? 77% extremely satisfied, 8% satisfied, 8% neutral, 8% dissatisfied
Would you join us again? 92% yes
Which workshop would you join? 46% weekly · 46% in-store private · 8% off-site
Which scent would you buy first? Lavender Lemonade led, then Bouquet and Rosemary + Lemon
13 responses from 15 attendees. Free-text answers named the ingredient explanation, the product story and the presentation itself as what worked.

Reading it honestly

The demand signal is real but small: 15 people, one post, no budget. What the survey does show is that the session changed intent — 92% said they would come back, and the workshop preference split almost evenly between the weekly class and the private in-store format, which is the more valuable one to sell. The single dissatisfied response is in the data too, and it is what the "what I would do differently" card above is answering.
