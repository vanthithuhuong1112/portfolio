Overview

The brief, and what I decided to do with it

The brand

Slow North is a small, woman-owned business in Austin, Texas making sustainable, non-toxic wellness products. Its core offer is a "Clean Burn" experience — natural fragrance blends built from soy wax and pure essential oils, plus hands-on candle-making workshops.

Home & living goods US market Sustainability DTC + workshops

The objective I set

  • Introduce the brand and its natural-ingredient story to a Vietnamese audience who had not encountered it.
  • Present the seasonal and festive candle line-up, plus a 50% discount on workshop attendance as the conversion incentive.
  • Drive registrations through a single organic Facebook promotion, then measure whether the audience actually showed up and whether they were satisfied.
Process

Before, during, after — the agenda I ran to

Before the event

Planning · promotion · technical setup
  • Confirmed the topic, built the registration form, designed the brochure, and set the session timing.
  • Promoted the webinar on Facebook using the brochure creative, then tracked registrations as they came in.
  • Created the event on Microsoft Teams Webinar and checked audio, video and notification settings before going live.
  • Set an automatic confirmation email to fire on every registration.
Brochure design Facebook promo MS Teams setup Email automation

During the event

Delivered live, in English
  • Opened with the purpose of the session, then walked through the brand overview and mission.
  • Covered the ingredient story — soy wax and essential oils including lavender, lemon, jasmine, rosemary, cinnamon, sandalwood and cedar.
  • Presented seven signature products — Lavender Lemonade, Tuscan Sun, Bouquet, Hello Sunshine, Merry + Bright, Autumn Woods and Cabin Retreat — and the three workshop formats.
  • Shared contact routes (website, Instagram, address, hotline) and facilitated a live Q&A.
English presenting Product storytelling Q&A facilitation

After the event

Measurement · documentation
  • Sent a feedback survey to attendees to measure satisfaction and gather qualitative reactions.
  • Evaluated whether the session met its objective and recorded what to change for a future run.
  • Most respondents reported being satisfied, left positive reviews, and said they would join an upcoming webinar.
Survey design Satisfaction analysis Post-event review

What I would do differently

Honest review
  • One organic Facebook post carried the whole funnel — a small paid boost would have widened the top of it considerably.
  • I measured satisfaction but not workshop redemption; tracking how many attendees used the 50% offer would have closed the loop to revenue.
  • A reminder email the day before would likely have lifted the show-up rate above 88%.
Funnel thinking Conversion tracking
Results

What the campaign produced

  • The Facebook promotion reached mainly a young audience and held stable interaction through the registration window.
  • 17 people registered through the form, each receiving an automated confirmation email.
  • 15 attended live on 27 August 2024 — an 88% show-up rate, which is high for a free online session.
  • Survey responses showed the majority were satisfied, gave positive feedback on the content, and expressed interest in attending a future webinar.
