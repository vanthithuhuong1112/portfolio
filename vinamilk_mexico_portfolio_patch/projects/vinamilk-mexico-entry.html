Overview

What the project asked

The group project assessed the feasibility of entering the Mexican dairy market and built a practical market-entry framework for Vinamilk. The report combined external market analysis, cultural and company analysis, entry-mode selection, strategic partnerships, financial considerations and an implementation roadmap.

Market evidence → commercial recommendation

Research & analysis

How the Mexico opportunity was structured

1. PESTEL

  • Reviewed trade-policy support and CPTPP-related market-access advantages.
  • Considered inflation, purchasing power and exchange-rate exposure.
  • Mapped health-conscious consumption, urbanisation and demand for convenient dairy products.
  • Covered food-safety, labelling, sustainability and technology factors affecting market entry.

2. Demand & consumer behaviour

  • Compared fresh, powdered and condensed milk categories using market size, growth and share estimates.
  • Used a BCG Matrix to structure which categories looked most attractive for entry.
  • Reviewed purchase drivers such as nutrition, convenience, price, family use and product quality.

3. Competitive landscape

  • Mapped Grupo Lala, Nestlé Mexico, Alpura and Santa Clara across major dairy segments.
  • Reviewed how distribution strength, brand loyalty, premium positioning and product breadth shape competition.
  • Identified health-focused, value-added dairy and modern retail/e-commerce as important market trends.

Proposed strategy

Enter with lower commitment, learn, then scale

Entry mode

  • Multidomestic orientation: adapt flavours, packaging and communication to Mexican preferences.
  • Export first: use Vietnam production capacity and test the market before committing to a local entity.
  • Sea freight: selected for lower unit cost for bulk consumer goods, while recognising lead-time and shelf-life trade-offs.

Strategic alliance

  • Proposed Walmart and Soriana as distribution partners to gain retail reach without building a network from zero.
  • Used retailer media and co-promotion as part of the market-awareness plan.
  • Recommended local influencers/KOLs to support product discovery and gather feedback on flavour and packaging.

Phased rollout

0–6 months: small-volume export pilot Test taste, packaging and consumer response 6–12 months: expand partner-store distribution Increase media and promotion Longer term: evaluate local joint venture if traction is sustained
Financial considerations

Cost, price and revenue logic

Selected cost assumptions

  • COGS: 15.54% of revenue
  • Logistics: 12%
  • Human resources: 8%
  • Marketing: 5%
  • Financial / exchange-rate contingency: 5%
  • Total projected cost structure: 64.74%

Pricing recommendation

The report recommended a penetration pricing strategy: use Vinamilk's production-cost advantage, tariff support and export economics to enter at a competitive price, accelerate trial and build early market access while monitoring margin stability.

Outcome

What the recommendation tied together

The final recommendation linked market attractiveness, category demand, competitive pressure, cultural adaptation, distribution access, cost structure and pricing into a phased plan designed to reduce commitment while Vinamilk learned from real consumer response.

For me, the project strengthened the ability to turn scattered market information into a structured business case: research → compare → cost → recommend.
